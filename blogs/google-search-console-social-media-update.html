Google Search Console Now Supports Social Media
Everything You Need to Know

By Bamboost Team August 3, 2026 6 min read Google Search Console

For years, if you posted a Reel on Instagram or dropped a new video on YouTube, you had exactly one way to know how it performed: the platform's own dashboard. Likes, views, watch time - all of it lived inside a walled garden. What you couldn't see was whether that same post was showing up in Google Search or Discover, or which search queries were actually leading people to it. If you didn't own a website, Search Console had nothing to offer you at all.

That gap has now closed. Google has rolled out platform properties, a new property type inside Google Search Console built specifically for social and video content. Here's what changed, how it works, and - more importantly - how businesses and SEO professionals should actually use it.

What Is the Google Search Console Social Media Update?

Google introduced platform properties in July 2026, and by the end of that month had rolled the feature out globally to every Search Console account. In simple terms, it lets you verify a social or video account - rather than a website - and see how that account's posts perform specifically inside Google Search, Discover, and Google News.

This is a genuinely new capability, not a cosmetic update. Search Console has always been built around domain ownership. Platform properties are the first property type that skip the domain entirely and report on content you don't host on your own site.

What Are Platform Properties?

A platform property is a verified connection between your social media account and Search Console. Once connected, it behaves much like a regular property: you get a Performance report showing clicks, impressions, and the exact queries triggering visibility, plus an Insights report that surfaces top and trending content at a glance.

The distinction worth remembering is this: platform properties are private analytics for you, not a public profile. Google separately launched "search profiles" earlier in 2026 as shareable, public pages - a different tool built for a different audience.

Supported Social Media Platforms

At launch, four platforms are supported, covering both social and video content:

  • Instagram: track Reels and posts that surface in Search or Discover.
  • X (formerly Twitter): monitor which posts and threads earn search visibility.
  • YouTube: see which videos appear in Search results and Google News.
  • TikTok: understand how short-form video content is discovered through Google.

Google has indicated more platforms could be added over time, though nothing beyond these four has been confirmed.

How Does It Work?

Platform properties don't index anything new or change how content ranks. They simply report on visibility that was already happening. Google's systems already surface social and video posts in Search and Discover results; this feature just gives creators and brands a window into that activity that didn't exist before.

Two limitations are worth flagging upfront. First, there's no historical backfill - your reporting begins the moment you verify the account, not before. Second, Discover and News data will only appear if a post actually received traffic from those surfaces, so a quiet account may show a mostly empty report at first.

How to Connect Your Social Media Accounts

Setup takes only a few minutes and needs no technical skills:

  1. Open Google Search Console and click the property selector dropdown.
  2. Select "Add property," then choose the platform property option.
  3. Pick the platform - Instagram, X, YouTube, or TikTok.
  4. Follow the on-screen prompts to authorise and verify the account.
  5. Wait for data to populate; initial reporting can take a few days to appear.

Why Can't Everyone Access It Yet?

Even though the rollout is now global, availability can still feel uneven for a short while after any staged Google release. If the option isn't visible in your account yet, it's rarely a setup mistake - it's more likely a rollout timing issue. Checking back over the following days usually resolves it.

Why This Update Matters for SEO

To be clear: connecting a social account to Search Console does not directly improve your Google rankings. What it does is remove a measurement blind spot that has existed since social platforms became major discovery channels.

Before this update, SEO strategy and social strategy were often measured in two completely separate systems, with no shared data connecting them. Now, an SEO professional can see whether a client's Instagram content is actually earning search impressions, and adjust content strategy based on real query data rather than guesswork. That's a meaningful upgrade to how technical SEO and content strategy work together.

How This Supports AI Search & Brand Visibility

This update also matters beyond traditional blue-link search. Google's AI Overviews, and AI models more broadly, tend to favour brands that show up consistently and credibly across multiple platforms - a core idea behind AI Search Optimization. Platform properties give you visibility into exactly how your brand is being discovered across social channels through Google, which is useful data for strengthening entity signals that AI systems rely on when deciding whether a brand is trustworthy enough to reference. It's a measurement layer that complements, rather than replaces, the fundamentals covered in our guide on AEO vs traditional SEO.

Best Practices

  • Verify every supported social account you actively post on, even if you're unsure how much search traffic it gets.
  • Check the Insights report regularly to spot which content formats are trending up in Search.
  • Compare platform property data against your website property to see where audiences overlap.
  • Keep brand naming and descriptions consistent across platforms to support entity recognition, as covered in our guide on how Google ranks websites.
  • Give it a few days after verification before drawing conclusions from thin data.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Assuming connecting an account will directly boost website rankings - it won't.
  • Expecting historical data - reporting only starts from the point of verification.
  • Treating platform properties as a replacement for your website's Search Console property.
  • Ignoring the data simply because a post shows zero Discover or News traffic - it only appears when that traffic actually occurs.

What This Means for the Future of SEO

Platform properties are a signal of where Google is heading: treating brands as entities that exist across many surfaces, not just a single website. As SEO continues to evolve alongside AI-powered search, expect more tools that measure a brand's presence holistically rather than page by page. Businesses that start building a habit of connecting analytics across channels now will have a clearer picture when the next update arrives.

Conclusion

Google Search Console's platform properties finally connect a measurement gap that social media managers, agencies, and brands have lived with for years. It won't move your rankings on its own, but it gives you real query-level visibility into how Instagram, X, YouTube, and TikTok content performs in Google Search - data that's genuinely useful for shaping content strategy and strengthening brand visibility across an increasingly AI-driven search landscape.
